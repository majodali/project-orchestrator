delivery modes, human-gated)'s pre-ratified sequence. Each role meets
the stop conditions it can trigger in its own contract.

## Approach

### The text stays, and the records change

P3-N013's readings agreed on draft 4 of the predicate text, commit
`fcf4894`. Criterion I7 makes any change to that text a revision both
readings must re-score. So this node copies the text unchanged
(decision 3).

The routed Backlog rows name points where the text is hard to apply
to past results. This node answers them on the record side. The loop
and the roles write each record in the words the predicates already
match. A past result could not be changed. A future one can.

### What the *Sequence mode* section says

The section sits beside the existing loop and changes none of it
(criterion B12). It states:

1. **Ratification.** Only the owner ratifies a sequence, at a gate.
   The ratification record holds `dispatch.md`'s scope-grant fields
   and one line of accepted risks, stated "none" when there are none
   (parent decision 13). It lists each member's node and each stage,
   one by one, never "up to" a gate. It lists each additional
   repository by its full name. The record is a section headed
   "Sequence ratified, <date>" in the document of the node whose gate
   ratified it. The Orchestrator journals `sequence-ratified` before
   the first member's `dispatched` event.
2. **The run.** The Orchestrator dispatches the next ratified member
   in the record's order, one at a time (parent plan decision 6). It
   never ratifies, extends or infers a sequence, register order
   included. No gate opens between members.
3. **Landing.** For each member the Orchestrator runs the acceptance
   checks, then applies the four predicates. A member whose status is
   not `done`, whose checks fail, or whose work lies outside the
   ratified scope is not accepted. Those are today's rules, applied
   unchanged. A member stopping on predicate 3's other tests or on
   predicate 4 has landed, so it is accepted first.
4. **The predicates**, copied byte for byte (decision 3). A lead-in
   outside the copied text maps its words: an item is a member's task,
   and the readings are whoever applies the text, the Orchestrator at
   landing or the owner at audit. Its mentions of the corpus statement
   point at the record.
5. **Stops.** The sequence stops on any of W-001's four conditions,
   and on the loop's existing step-4 stops: an empty actionable set, a
   blocked task, and exhausted scope. The end of the ratified list is
   exhausted scope. No other stop is added (parent decision 14).
6. **Stop, summarize, wait.** The Orchestrator journals
   `sequence-stopped` and writes a stop summary in the gate summary's
   template. The summary carries the stopping member's asks only. It
   names every ratified member not yet dispatched as `gated` (no
   silent drops). Asks are never rolled up across members.
7. **The obligation table** (criterion B2, I8). One row per MUST and
   MUST NOT of W-001's sequence text, each mapped to the sentence that
   carries it. The release register names no change to W-001 or W-008
   after v1.6.0. The table quotes the pinned text with a provenance
   line.
8. **The record of a member's result** (below).
9. **The service.** Nothing in the mode needs the orchestration
   service (I12).

The gate record form is stated beside the *Gates* section, by
addition: a section headed "Gate record, <date>" adopts or overrides
each decision by its number or by a range of numbers. Predicate 3's
test 2 reads that form without doubt.

### The member's result record

Parent decision 12 puts the result's status and its Asks part,
verbatim, in the `result-received` event. Predicates 1 to 3 read more
than that. A record without their inputs stops every member at audit,
because each predicate fails closed. P3-N013's pre-pass found an
outcome for the form check on 13 of 53 past items.

So the record carries every input the four predicates read
(decision 4). It is the `note` of the `result-received` event, in a
fixed order:

1. The status token, first, as predicate 3's test 1 reads it.
2. *Checks*: each named check, with one outcome word from predicate
   1's pass or failure list.
3. *Refs*: each commit with its repository's full name.
4. *Flags*: "none", or each immediate-class matter in the words
   "immediate class" with its class.
5. *Findings*: the located findings field, verbatim.
6. *What changed*: the result's What changed part, verbatim, or
   "none".
7. *Asks*: the Asks part verbatim, or its stated empty form. Where
   the result carries an intermediate representation (IR), the IR's
   asks.

The `accepted` note opens with the outcome of the Orchestrator's own
form-check run. The section states that a predicate verdict is never
written into a note of the member's task. Such a note would put the
predicate's own words beside the check names it matches.

### The handoff contract and the owner's clarification

The result core's asks part (item 3) changes in three ways.

- Each ask names its addressee, the owner or the Orchestrator. An ask
  naming neither counts as owner-addressed (parent decision 11).
- An ask addressed to the Orchestrator requests only what the
  Orchestrator discharges at acceptance under the loop's step 3:
  register changes, Backlog additions, records, and the merge of the
  task's own branch. Any other such ask fails the handoff check
  (decision 6).
- The clarification of 2026-09-17 is restated. An Asks part holds
  only what its reader can act on now. It may hold an open question
  the reader can answer on the spot. An open question waiting on other
  activity, and an item the report says needs no answer yet, go in a
  *What changed* part. A result holding such a question carries that
  part. W-008 (reports map their deliverables) lets an agent-audience
  report include it.

The deliverable map names the repository of each commit by its full
name. The gate summary template gains the same Asks rule
(decision 5).

### The journal

Two event kinds are added to [observability.md](../process/observability.md)
and to the `EVENTS` set in `plugin/scripts/lib/form-check-core.ts`.

- `sequence-ratified`. The `node` is the node whose document holds the
  ratification record, and the `ref` is its commit. The `note` lists
  the members.
- `sequence-stopped`. The `task` and `node` are the stopping member's.
  The `note` opens with the condition, 1 to 4, or the step-4 stop. It
  then carries the stopping member's asks verbatim.

The form checker's conformance corpus gains one fixture whose journal
uses both kinds without a finding. No existing fixture or expectation
changes.

The *Position* paragraph of observability.md says that nothing in the
process may depend on reading the journal back. Parent decision 12
makes the `result-received` event the audit record the owner reads at
criterion G2. The paragraph gains the exception: the stop is decided
in session, and the record lets it be checked afterwards. It also
states that a `note` may be long when it carries a member's result
record.

### The execute task, and its acceptance

One Implementer task, E1, on the advanced tier (decision 10). It
writes every file under *Repository reach*. It reads the contracts at
its branch base and names that commit (B10). It runs
`node plugin/scripts/sync_agents.ts`, then `--check`,
`node plugin/scripts/form_check.ts` and
`node plugin/scripts/run_corpus.ts`. It self-verifies against B1 to
B12 and the additions below (C1 profile).

After the acceptance commit, the Orchestrator tries the record form on
E1 itself (decision 9). It writes E1's `result-received` note in the
member's form. It applies the four predicates and re-runs the
pre-pass, `node docs/findings/p3-n005-stop-predicates/prepass.ts`,
with the acceptance commit as its cut-off. It states the four verdicts
in this node's Backlog entry and in its report, never in a note of
E1. A verdict resting on missing input is a defect of the form. It
sends the node back to `executing` with that reason.
