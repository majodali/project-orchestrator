## Outcome

This project's own dispatch loop runs methodology **W-001**'s
pre-ratified sequence mode, and the four conditions that stop a
sequence are applied mechanically by the roles that meet them.

Concretely, at `done`:

- [dispatch.md](../process/dispatch.md) carries a normative
  *Sequence mode* section: how a sequence is ratified, what the loop
  does between chunks, what stops it, and what a stop produces.
- The four stop conditions exist as **predicates** — each naming the
  artifact and the field or section it reads, and yielding stop or
  continue without a fresh judgment.
- The predicates are written into the six role contracts in
  `.claude/agents/`, in each role's own terms, with `plugin/agents/`
  regenerated by `node plugin/scripts/sync_agents.ts` — so a
  dispatched role meets them without looking anything up.
- The predicates are **scored against this project's own past task
  results**, by two independent readings that agree on every verdict.
- An owner-ratified sequence has run in this repository: at least two
  consecutive chunks landed with no per-chunk gate between them, then
  a chunk whose result carried asks stopped it, with the stop and its
  reason in the run journal.
- The determination on whether W-001 needs specialising upstream is
  recorded — as an Article 8 proposal in [RU-015](../rulings.md)'s
  form, or as a reasoned argument that no amendment is needed.

The node's criteria in the parent specification are **G6**, **I8**
and **P4**.

## What this node is, and what it is not

**It does not invent a delivery mode.** W-001 already gives two modes,
both human-gated, and Allegro has run the pre-ratified one on three of
four lanes since deviation D-1 of 2026-09-01. This project implements
only W-001's other mode — the per-chunk gate — and the parent plan
gives the gap no other home. The work is to run the mode the
methodology already permits.

**Only the maintainer pre-ratifies.** That is not a constraint to
design around; it is the property that keeps the mode human-gated. The
Orchestrator may not ratify a sequence, extend one, or infer one from
register order. A sequence that runs out of ratified chunks stops.

**Asks are never rolled up across chunks.** When a chunk's result
carries asks, the sequence stops at that chunk. The loop does not
carry asks forward to report them together at the end — that is
exactly the behaviour the condition exists to prevent.

The optional upstream half is deliberately weaker than the local half.
Criterion **I8** says a well-argued *"no mechanism, and here is the
clarification that would have been one"* satisfies it in full, and the
owner judges an amendment likely **only as a clarification of the
interruption criteria**, not as a new mode. This plan does not force a
mechanism into existence.

## The four stop conditions, and what makes each decidable

W-001 stops a sequence on all four. Three are close to mechanical
already in this project's artifacts; the third is the one criterion
**I8** names as an ambiguity when an agent reads it. The predicates
below are the plan's proposed starting text, not its output — child A
tests and revises them before anything else depends on them.

| W-001 condition | Proposed predicate | What it reads |
|---|---|---|
| A failed check | The acceptance step's checks do not all pass: `node plugin/scripts/form_check.ts` returns non-zero or reports a violation; a declared build, test or lint command fails; self-verification against the node's criteria returns any criterion not met | Command exit status; the verification result |
| A scope change | The result names work outside the approved scope's nodes and stages, or a repository the node's plan does not declare ([RU-016](../rulings.md)); or the result raises anything in `dispatch.md`'s **immediate class** — scope expansion, a W-002 test-condition change, a deviation, budget exhaustion | The task result; the scope grant; the node's plan |
| A chunk needing a maintainer decision | Any task result for the chunk has status `needs-judgment`; **or** the chunk's plan or specification carries a *Decisions for the gate* entry not yet adopted or overridden at a gate | The task result's status field; the node's documents |
| A landing summary carrying asks | The result's W-008 *Asks* part is non-empty — anything other than the stated empty part — after the owner's 2026-09-17 clarification is applied | The result's named *Asks* section |

Three things about this table are load-bearing.

**"A chunk needing a maintainer decision" becomes a lookup.** This
project already requires every owner decision to be a numbered entry
with a default in a *Decisions for the gate* section
([roles.md](../process/roles.md), `dispatch.md`'s owner-decision
economics), and requires a role meeting a decision above its authority
to return `needs-judgment` rather than decide. So the judgment W-001
leaves to a human reader is already recorded in two places a predicate
can read. That is the specialisation this project is placed to make,
and it is the strongest candidate for the upstream clarification.

**"Landing summary" needs a local definition.** W-001 says landing
summary; this project says task result. A chunk's landing summary is
the **W-008-shaped task result of the task that completed the chunk**,
and the *Asks* predicate reads its named *Asks* part. Without that
mapping the condition has no artifact to read here.

**The owner's 2026-09-17 clarification changes which results stop a
sequence, and it removes a stop unless condition 3 catches it.** An
*Asks* section carries only direct asks the reader can act on right
now; it may include an open question answerable on the spot; it must
not include an open question waiting on some other activity. That
narrows *Asks*, so results that would have stopped a sequence under a
loose reading no longer do — their open questions move to *What
changed*. Where such a question is genuinely a maintainer decision,
condition 3 must catch it, or the clarification loses a stop the mode
needs. The two conditions are therefore specified and scored together,
never separately. The Backlog already owns this clarification and
records it as bound upstream; see decision 5.

## Where the conditions are written, and why there

The parent specification's addition (i) is explicit: the stop
conditions go **where a dispatched role meets them without looking**,
not only in a spec document a role may not read.

This project has a recorded encounter that settles the point.
[R17](../open-risks.md) — the harness's own guidance pushing against
the fallback rule — found a session under live pressure to do the
opposite of this project's no-retry rule, and its mitigation says the
rule *"cannot be stated once in a document a session may not have
read"*. The R12 exercise proved it: `enlistment.md` was absent from the
branch under test and the session proceeded on the rule as described to
it. A stop condition stated only in `dispatch.md` has the same defect.

So the text lands in three places with one source:

1. **[dispatch.md](../process/dispatch.md)** — normative. A *Sequence
   mode* section beside the existing loop, ratification and all.
2. **[roles.md](../process/roles.md)** — which role evaluates which
   condition, inside each role's existing *decides* and *routes*
   lists.
3. **`.claude/agents/`** — the six role contracts, each carrying the
   conditions it can trigger, in its own terms. `plugin/agents/` is
   regenerated from them by `node plugin/scripts/sync_agents.ts`; the
   generated copies are never hand-edited ([RU-012](../rulings.md)).

The split by role, proposed:

- **Orchestrator** — evaluates all four at acceptance; runs the
  ratified sequence; never ratifies or extends one; journals the stop
  with its condition.
- **Implementer, Planner, Reviewer** — the *Asks* discipline in the
  result they write, and the knowledge that a `needs-judgment` return
  or a non-empty *Asks* part stops a running sequence. Stating the
  consequence matters: a role that does not know it is padding *Asks*
  with things the reader cannot act on will pad it.
- **Auditor, Semantic Auditor** — unchanged in substance; a finding is
  delivered on the audit channel, not as a sequence stop.

## Evidence: applying the conditions the same way twice

Criterion **I8** requires the conditions scored against a set of this
project's own past task results, with **two independent readings
producing the same stop-or-continue verdict on each**.

**The corpus.** The [Cost log](../cost-log.md) carries **38 rows**
(T001–T039, less T017, which returned `needs-judgment` and got no row
of its own — the Backlog owns that gap). Counting T017 from the run
journal gives **39 task results**. The parent specification's
"thirty-eight" counts the Cost log's rows; both figures describe the
same history, and the node states which it used rather than picking
one silently.

**The corpus is asymmetric, and the plan says so rather than hiding
it.** Conditions 1–3 are scorable across all 39: status, check
outcomes and staged decisions are recorded for every task. Condition 4
is not. W-008 reached this project at the v1.5.0 migration of
2026-09-02, so most of the history has no *Asks* section to read, and
scoring one against a result that never had one would be invention.
Condition 4 is therefore scored over the subset written under W-008 —
T039 onward, plus this round's parallel plan tasks and the
demonstration's own results — with the coverage gap recorded. See
decision 3.

**How two readings are obtained without one reader seeing the
other's.** Instruction is not a mechanism; artifact separation is.

- Reading 1 is a dispatched session given the predicate text and the
  corpus, and nothing else. It commits its verdicts as a file.
- Reading 2 is a **second dispatched session on a different model
  tier**, dispatched from a branch **cut before reading 1's commit**.
  Reading 1's file does not exist in its tree, so blindness is a
  property of the checkout rather than of a promise. Its packet
  enumerates the same predicate text and the same corpus.
- Where the record is structured — a journal `result-received` status,
  a form-check outcome — a **mechanical pre-pass** scores it, and both
  readings are checked against it. This is supplementary evidence, not
  a third reading.

Two readings drawn from the same model family can agree by correlation
rather than by the text being clear. Different tiers reduce that;
they do not remove it, and the node records the limitation rather than
claiming more than it has.

**Disagreement is the useful outcome.** A verdict mismatch localizes an
ambiguity in the predicate text. The predicate is sharpened, the
revision is recorded with the disagreement that caused it, and both
readings re-run. Bounded at **two rounds** by `dispatch.md`'s
bounded-attempts rule; a condition still disagreeing after two is
escalated rather than re-run, and it is evidence for the upstream
half.

**The predicates are fixed before either reading.** Criteria exist
before the work they verify ([plan-model.md](../process/plan-model.md)).
Any change to a predicate after a reading is a recorded revision that
invalidates both readings, which is what stops the text from being
tuned to match an answer already seen.

## The demonstration (G6), which is performed rather than argued

**G6** is a live demonstration. It needs real chunks to land, and this
project's Backlog is full of small owned findings.

The sequence: **at least three chunks**, ratified once by the owner.
Two land back to back with no gate between them. The third carries a
genuine act-on-now ask in its result, and the sequence stops there.

**The stop must be genuine, not staged.** "Induced deliberately" means
the third chunk is chosen because its work really does produce an ask
the owner can act on immediately — not because an ask was written into
a result to trip the predicate. A manufactured ask would demonstrate
the predicate firing and nothing about the mode.

Candidate payload, all owned Backlog findings in this repository:

- The preflight wording [R16](../open-risks.md) records as still to
  fix — a real code change with a real test.
- The editorial reconciliation of the two open P3-N001 Backlog
  entries, which the Backlog explicitly leaves for the owner to
  sequence.
- The `docs/process/` methodology citations still targeting `v1.3.0`,
  which the Backlog already owns.
- A chunk whose result asks the owner to hand-carry an upstream
  artifact ([RU-002](../rulings.md)) — the natural source of a genuine
  act-on-now ask, and a candidate for the third position.

Selection is child C's, against a stated rule: each item already owned
in the Backlog, each leaf-sized, each landing inside this node's
declared repository reach, mutually independent so that a stop leaves
the tree clean, and the last one genuinely producing an ask. The
selected sequence is the object of the owner's ratification, so it
appears in the gate summary before it runs.

**The payload items become register nodes.** A sequence lands chunks,
and a chunk is a node. They are entered as children of child C at the
ratification gate, and their Backlog entries move with them. The cut
inside child C is therefore **not feature-first**, and this is
deliberate and recorded: what is being validated is the loop, and the
payload is chosen to be incidental. A feature-first cut there would
make the demonstration a feature of whichever finding it happened to
land.

## The lost-update race on the Backlog, which this node must answer

Three plan tasks ran concurrently under [RU-018](../rulings.md) on
2026-09-17, and doing so exposed a hazard no rule covers: every role
writes its own Backlog additions in the same commit as its work
(W-003, founding ruling 1), and two roles running at once both read
`docs/backlog.md`, both append, and the second write silently drops
the first. It is filed in the Backlog against **P1-N016** and named as
live input here.

**The answer, stated either way as the brief requires.**

*Sequence mode does not create the race.* The race needs two
concurrent writers. A sequence is serial by construction — chunks land
one after another, and acceptance is serial regardless
(`dispatch.md`'s single-writer rule). What sequence mode removes is
the human who would have noticed a lost write at the next gate, so
what it adds is **detection latency, not the race**.

*And no check catches it.* A lost Backlog *addition* — a finding that
was never written — is invisible to `form_check.ts`, which cannot miss
what was never there. Claiming the failed-check condition covers it
would be false.

*So this node owes two things, both small.* First, the *Sequence mode*
section states that **within a ratified sequence the Orchestrator
dispatches one task at a time**: a sequence is never run with parallel
dispatch of its own members, so the two modes do not compound. Second,
the node records that this constrains sequence mode only, and that the
general rule for parallel dispatch — the Orchestrator owning the
Backlog whenever dispatch is parallel, or per-task fragments it merges
— stays with **P1-N016**, which already owns it. Duplicating that rule
here would create a second statement of one contract, which is the
drift hazard this project keeps legislating against.

Decision 6 states it so the owner can override it.

## The orchestration service

Sequence mode needs **nothing** from it. The mode is loop policy,
role-contract text and a journal event kind. Every part of it works
with the service unreachable, which is the standing position: the
service is an accelerator, never a dependency (orchestration service
plan, constraint 3; [R10](../open-risks.md), [R12](../open-risks.md)).

The service's own **P2-N005 task-pull loop** is where sequence mode
would later be accelerated — a ratified sequence is exactly the kind
of queue a pull loop serves. That is a later service chunk's work and
this node states the relationship without creating one.

## Leaf or interior

**Interior.** Four children. Two halves plus a live demonstration is
not one chunk: the predicates must be evidenced before they are
written into eight documents; the demonstration cannot run until the
loop can run it; and the determination must not be written before the
mode has actually run, which is the parent specification's own
rationale for decision 3 — *a determination about a mode nobody has
run here would be decided on paper*.

The decomposition is not feature-first, and the reason is recorded.
This node has one feature — the loop runs a ratified sequence — and it
is atomic in the plan-model's sense: no sub-behaviour of it validates
separately. What separates the children is **evidence**: what must be
true, and proven, before the next child may start. Children A and B
are the feature; C is its demonstration; D is the determination the
first three make answerable.

Child A leads because it is the thin slice in the only sense available
here. The structural risk in this node is not the loop policy, which
is ten paragraphs of prose; it is that **the stop conditions cannot be
made decidable**. Child A runs that risk first, against a deliberately
small sample, before eight documents and a live demonstration depend
on the answer ([plan-model](../process/plan-model.md)'s thin-slice
guidance, [R8](../open-risks.md)).

**Dependency order.** A → B → C → D, strictly. B writes text A must
have evidenced; C runs the loop B built; D argues from what C showed.
No two of these are independent, so nothing here is marked
independent under RU-018.

## Verification criteria for this node

The plan-stage statement; the specify stage refines it into the
checkable form, and that document will be authoritative.

The chunk is complete when:

1. Each of W-001's four stop conditions exists as a predicate naming
   the artifact and field it reads, and none requires judging prose.
2. The predicates are scored against this project's own past task
   results, and two independent readings — blind by checkout
   separation, on different tiers — return the same stop-or-continue
   verdict on every item in each condition's corpus.
3. The corpus, including condition 4's asymmetry and its coverage
   gap, is recorded with the scoring.
4. The conditions are stated in `dispatch.md`, in `roles.md` per role,
   and in the six `.claude/agents/` contracts, with `plugin/agents/`
   regenerated and matching.
5. The *Asks* predicate reads W-008's named section and applies the
   owner's 2026-09-17 clarification; conditions 3 and 4 are specified
   together so the narrowing does not lose a stop.
6. An owner-ratified sequence in this repository lands at least two
   consecutive chunks with no per-chunk gate between them, then stops
   on a chunk whose result carries a genuine act-on-now ask, with the
   stop and its reason in the run journal. Only the owner ratified,
   at any point.
7. The run journal can record a sequence stop with its condition, and
   did.
8. The *Sequence mode* section states that dispatch inside a ratified
   sequence is serial, and records that the general parallel-dispatch
   Backlog rule stays with P1-N016.
9. The determination is recorded either way, and any upstream artifact
   is in RU-015's form.
10. `node plugin/scripts/form_check.ts` passes clean; the Backlog and
    registers moved in the same commits as the work (W-003).

Criterion 6 is the owner-facing gate. The rest are the conditions that
make it mean something.

## Decisions for the gate

Numbered per [dispatch.md](../process/dispatch.md)'s owner-decision
economics; the owner's go-ahead adopts every default not overridden by
number. This document opens its own sequence at 1.

**Precedents checked against the [Ruling register](../rulings.md).**
Five active rulings are exact matches and decide silently rather than
being re-raised: **RU-002** (upstream-bound output is hand-carried by
the owner), **RU-003** (the node completes at the artifact, not at
upstream disposition), **RU-005** (proposal-class nodes are owner-gated
at `verifying` — child D), **RU-015** (the delivery form for a change
to a repository this project cannot write to), and **RU-016** (the
Planner declares repository reach in the plan; the service repository
is permanently in scope). **RU-018** is the authority for this node's
independence from P3-N002, and **RU-012** for the
`.claude/agents/` → `plugin/agents/` generation direction.

1. **Where the stop conditions live normatively.** Default:
   **`dispatch.md` is normative**; `roles.md` states which role
   evaluates which condition; the six `.claude/agents/` contracts
   carry them in each role's own terms, with `plugin/agents/`
   regenerated. Rationale: the spec is authoritative and the contracts
   are the surface a dispatched role actually loads —
   [R17](../open-risks.md)'s recorded lesson that a rule stated once

2. **How the two independent readings are obtained.** Default: **two

   see each other, and blindness enforced by the checkout is checkable

3. **The corpus, and its asymmetry.** Default: conditions 1–3 scored

   condition 4 scored over the subset written under W-008 — T039
   onward plus the demonstration's own results — with the coverage gap
   recorded. Rationale: W-008 postdates most of this history, and
   scoring an *Asks* section against results that never had one would
   be invention; I8 asks for evidence, not coverage theatre.
4. **What the ratified sequence is made of.** Default: **three or more
   owned Backlog findings in this repository**, entered as register
   nodes at the ratification gate, the last chosen because its work
   genuinely produces an act-on-now ask. Rationale: real chunks with
   no new repository grant, and a stop that is genuine rather than
   staged; a manufactured ask would demonstrate the predicate and
   nothing about the mode.
5. **Whether the upstream half also carries the W-008 *Asks*
   clarification.** Default: **yes** — two proposal documents in
   RU-015's form plus a cover note, following P1-N015's precedent.
   Rationale: the Backlog already owns that clarification, records it
   as bound upstream via Article 8, and says it is small enough to
   ride with the next amendment rather than travel alone; this is that
   amendment, and it is the same interruption-criteria subject.
6. **What sequence mode does about the parallel-Backlog race.**
   Default: **a sequence is serial by construction** — within a
   ratified sequence the Orchestrator dispatches one task at a time —
   and the general parallel-dispatch rule stays with **P1-N016**.
   Rationale: the race needs two concurrent writers and a sequence has
   one, so sequence mode adds detection latency rather than the
   hazard; owning the general rule here would be a second statement of
   a contract another node already owns.
7. **Whether the node needs anything from the orchestration
   service.** Default: **no** — the mode is loop policy, contract text
   and a journal event kind, and it runs with the service unreachable.
   Rationale: constraint 3 and [R12](../open-risks.md); the service is
   an accelerator, never a dependency. P2-N005's task-pull loop may
   later accelerate a ratified sequence, which is a relationship this
   node states rather than creates.
8. **Ordering against P1-N014, which edits the same role contracts.**
   Default: **no ordering constraint** — both nodes may proceed, the
   later one reconciles, and child B reads the contracts as they then
   stand rather than as this plan describes them. Rationale: the two
   sets of additions do not overlap in content, P1-N014 is `planned`
   and unscheduled, and imposing an order would block this node on a
   node it does not depend on — the defect RU-018 exists to prevent.
   *Alternative the owner may prefer:* schedule P1-N014's execute
   before child B, so the contracts are touched once.
